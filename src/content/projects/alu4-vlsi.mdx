## What it does

Two 4-bit inputs, A and B, and one control bit, Sub:

- Sub = 0 gives S = A + B
- Sub = 1 gives S = B + ~A + 1, which is B minus A in two's complement

The four result bits go out through tri-state buffers, so with En low the outputs let go and another block can drive the same bus. Cout comes out too. When subtracting, Cout = 1 means B is at least A.

## Built from my own cells

Everything is made from a small standard cell set I drew earlier: NOT, NAND, XOR and a full adder built from two XORs and three NANDs. The one new cell is the tri-state buffer. Every transistor is the same size, 6 by 2 lambda on the MOSIS 0.6 um process (lambda = 0.3 um).

Each bit is the same three blocks in a row:

- an XOR of A with Sub, which passes A through when adding and inverts it when subtracting
- a full adder, with the carry rippling from bit to bit and Sub feeding the first carry in, which supplies the +1
- a tri-state buffer on the sum

## The tri-state buffer

Two inverters make D_bar and En_bar, and those drive a four-transistor stack: two PMOS in series from vdd and two NMOS in series to ground. With En high both outer transistors are on and the stack is just an inverter of D_bar, so the output equals D. With En low both outer transistors are off and the output floats.

## One row

The whole unit is a single row, 1202 by 72 um. Every cell sits on the same vdd and ground rails, so the rails run straight through from end to end. The routing follows one rule: metal 1 runs horizontally and metal 2 runs vertically, so anything that crosses a rail does it in metal 2 and can't short to power.

The inputs come in along the bottom edge, the outputs leave from the top, and the carry runs along the top of the row into the next slice. Sub and En are two long buses under the row.

## Checking it

Electric's LVS (NCC) says the layout matches the schematic for every cell, all the way up to the full unit.

The first test bench runs nine hand-picked vectors, 40 ns each: four additions, then five subtractions, then En goes low. To make the results readable I added three display-only sources that show A, B and S as plain numbers from 0 to 15.

<Figure src={img_demo} alt={"LTspice plot with panes for Sub and En, A and B as numbers, S as a number, then the four output bits and Cout"} caption={"Test vectors in LTspice. Reading across: 3+5=8, 7+9=0 carry 1, 15+1=0 carry 1, 10+10=4 carry 1, then 5-3=2, 3-5=14 (minus 2), 6-6=0, 9-0=9, 2-15=3. At 360 ns En drops and the outputs float"} />

The second counts through every combination of A, B and Sub, 512 vectors at 20 ns each, and checks the outputs just before each step. All 512 are right.

<Figure src={img_all} alt={"LTspice plot over 10 microseconds with Sub, A stepping and B ramping, S as a number, and Cout"} caption={"All 512 combinations: Sub, then A (red steps) and B (cyan ramps), then S and Cout"} />

## The layout, simulated

Then I ran the same tests on the netlist Electric pulls back out of the layout. That netlist includes the source and drain area of every transistor, which adds capacitance the schematic leaves out. It still gets 512 out of 512 and the test vectors come out identical, but it is about twice as slow.

<Figure src={img_demo_lay} alt={"LTspice plot of the same test vectors run on the extracted layout netlist, matching the schematic"} caption={"The same test vectors on the extracted layout"} />

| | Schematic | Extracted layout |
|---|---|---|
| All 512 combinations | correct | correct |
| Slowest settle | 1.6 ns | 3.0 ns |
| Median settle | 0.75 ns | 1.6 ns |

The slowest case is a carry rippling through all four bits: subtracting 5 while B goes from 12 to 13, so the answer goes from 0111 to 1000. B0 rises at 1 ns, S0, S1 and S2 fall one after another as the carry moves up, and S3 rises last.

<Figure src={img_worst} alt={"LTspice plot of B0 rising at 1 ns, then S0, S1 and S2 falling in turn and S3 rising at about 2.6 ns"} caption={"Slowest transition, schematic: S3 settles about 1.6 ns after the edge"} />

<Figure src={img_worst_lay} alt={"The same transition on the extracted layout, with every output edge later and S3 rising at about 4 ns"} caption={"Same transition, extracted layout: about 3 ns"} />

## What I'd change

The cells are taller than they need to be. The space between the PMOS and NMOS rows holds about eight rows of gate wiring, and redrawing the cells with tighter wiring would cut maybe 10 to 15 percent off the height. A carry-lookahead adder would fix the ripple delay, but for four bits the ripple is already only a few nanoseconds.
