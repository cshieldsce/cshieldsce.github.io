## The board

The goal was a two-wheel robot that balances. The board has an STM32F401 at 84 MHz from a 16 MHz crystal, a TB6612FNG motor driver, an MPU6050 IMU on I2C, an MT6701 magnetic encoder on a second I2C bus, a WS2812 LED driven by a timer and DMA, and an ESP-01 for a Wi-Fi control page. It's two layers and about 68.65 by 53.48 mm.

<Figure src={img_pcb_top_render} alt={"KiCad render of the robot board with an STM32 in the middle, a motor driver, connectors for the encoder, IMU, battery and motors, and an ESP connector"} caption={"The layout I routed. The board that got built used the instructor's layout."} />

## What was mine

I hand-soldered the board, except the microcontroller and the USB connector, which the instructors did. I routed my own layout in KiCad from the course schematic, but the instructor's layout was the one that got built. I drew the chassis in CAD and exported a DXF for laser cutting. The firmware scheduler, telemetry and command code in `main.c` is mine: the IMU runs at 100 Hz through a complementary filter (coefficient 0.995), telemetry goes out every 100 ms as `$speed,roll,pitch,counter`, and drive commands are single bytes. The peripheral drivers came from the instructor and the IMU driver is a third-party library.

## What happened

It never ran firmware. The USB-C port never enumerated on my Linux machine, and `dmesg` just said `device descriptor read/64, error -71`. USB was the only way to program the board, so I couldn't flash it.

So I went in over SWD with the ST-Link on an STM32F4 Discovery board, jumpers pulled, wired to SWDIO, SWCLK, ground and 3.3 V. Both `STM32_Programmer_CLI` and `st-info` gave the same answer:

<Figure src={img_swd_debug_setup} alt={"The robot board wired with four jumpers to an STM32F4 Discovery board used as a programmer"} caption={"Debugging over SWD with a borrowed ST-Link"} />

```text
Voltage : 2.90V
Error: Unable to get core ID
Error: No STM32 target found!
```

I tried both wire orders, hot-plug and connect-under-reset, and both power sources. Same result. That rules out the programmer, the cable and my wiring. My diagnosis is that the chip was being phantom-powered through the protection diodes on its I/O pins while its real VDD net wasn't connected. In the photo the ESP-01, a blue LED and the IMU's red LED are lit, so some 3.3 V loads work. The suspect is the microcontroller's own VDD connection. I never probed the regulator or the VDD pins, so the cause is still a guess, and the balancing controller stayed a plan on paper.
