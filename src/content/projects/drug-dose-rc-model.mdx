## The model

The circuit is an electrical stand-in for how a drug moves through the body. C1 is the dose, R1 is the resistance of getting from the injection into the blood, C2 is the volume of blood in the region, and R2 is the body clearing it out through the kidneys and the like. I charge C1 to 20 V and close the switch, the way a nurse pushes a syringe, and watch the voltage on C2. That voltage is how much of the drug is in the bloodstream.

## Solving it

I wrote the node equations at C2 and for the current out of C1, and they collapse into one second-order equation. The roots are real and different, about -33.85 and -5.30 per second, so the response is overdamped:

```text
V(t) = -2.12 e^(-33.85 t) + 2.12 e^(-5.30 t)
```

At t = 0 all 20 V sits across R1 and C2 is empty, so C2 starts rising at 60.6 V/s. It climbs, peaks, and then drains away over about a second. From that equation I get a peak of about 1.27 V, around 65 ms in.
