## The board

A Terasic DE10-Lite with a MAX 10 FPGA, using Quartus Prime 25.1 Lite. The lab manuals were written for an older board with 18 switches and 8 displays, so I rescaled each lab to this one's 10 switches and 6 displays.

<Figure src={img_lab1_hello_board_crop} alt={"A DE10-Lite board with HELLO shown on its seven-segment displays"} caption={"Lab 1, HELLO on the displays"} />

## What I built

- **Lab 1:** an 8-bit 2-to-1 multiplexer, a 3-bit 5-to-1 multiplexer and a seven-segment decoder, then HELLO rotating across the displays with switches choosing the rotation. The whole thing compiles to 24 logic elements out of 49,760 and uses no registers.
- **Lab 2:** a two-digit hex decoder where I minimized each segment from a Karnaugh map, binary to decimal with a comparator (`z = v3 & (v2 | v1)`), a 4-bit ripple-carry adder from four full adders, and BCD adders. The BCD adders take operands from 0 to 19 and show A, B and the sum on separate displays.
- **Lab 3:** latches and flip-flops. A gated RS latch, a gated D latch, a master-slave D flip-flop, and a comparison of a behavioral latch against positive-edge and negative-edge flip-flops. The RS latch needs `/* synthesis keep */` or Quartus folds it into a single lookup table. Buttons are active low, so the clocks are `~KEY[n]`.
- **Lab 4:** counters. An 8-bit counter from T flip-flops and a 16-bit behavioral counter, both running off a 1 Hz clock divided down from the 50 MHz board clock (the divider toggles every 25,000,000 cycles). The HELLO ticker is an 11-state machine and compiles to 83 logic elements and 31 registers.
